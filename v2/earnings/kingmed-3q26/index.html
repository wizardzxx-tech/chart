3Q26 results: Revenue remained under pressure, while business mix and profitability continued to improve.

KingMed reported 3Q26 revenue of RMB1.52bn, down 1.5% YoY, bringing 9M26 revenue to RMB4.40bn, down 3.0% YoY. The revenue decline was mainly attributable to lower testing prices, weak demand for routine testing at lower-tier hospitals, and the company's proactive exit from low-margin businesses. Despite sample volume growth of over 2% YoY in 9M26, revenue per sample continued to decline. In addition, a high base from one-off COVID-related revenue in 3Q25 weighed on reported growth; excluding this impact, management indicated that 3Q26 revenue had returned to positive growth. Business mix continued to improve, supported by strong momentum in high-value testing and laboratory collaboration projects. Revenue from key specialty testing products grew 14.7% YoY in 3Q26, with the affordable testing product portfolio up 52.1%. In 9M26, laboratory collaboration revenue increased 8.9%, including 53% growth from precision diagnostic centers, alongside 20 newly established precision centers and 31 newly signed county-level medical consortium projects. Tier-3 hospitals accounted for 55.3% of revenue, reflecting continued customer mix improvement. Meanwhile, 3Q26 net profit reached RMB183mn, representing a sharp YoY recovery, while 9M26 gross margin expanded 1.9ppt YoY to 36.7%, supported by cost optimization, favorable business mix shifts, and lower credit impairment losses.

Outlook: Manageable pricing pressure, with specialty testing and outsourcing penetration supporting long-term growth.

Based on the five provinces that have implemented the latest medical testing fee reforms, management estimates an approximately 5% reduction in KingMed's overall testing prices on a static basis, broadly in line with its earlier expectation of relatively moderate pricing pressure. The company plans to mitigate the impact through cost sharing with upstream suppliers, product portfolio optimization, more disciplined customer-level discount management, and improved laboratory operating efficiency. Management believes standardized testing fees could facilitate hospital adoption of innovative diagnostic tests, encourage greater outsourcing of laboratory services, and accelerate industry consolidation in favor of leading players. Looking ahead, management expects the industry's policy adjustment cycle to gradually conclude in 2027, although some pricing pressure may persist. The company intends to sustain growth through high-end specialty testing, precision diagnostic centers, and county-level medical consortium projects, while leveraging AI and digitalization to enhance operational efficiency and reduce costs. Over the longer term, management sees opportunities to expand its market share through rising outsourcing penetration and continued consolidation of smaller independent clinical laboratories.
