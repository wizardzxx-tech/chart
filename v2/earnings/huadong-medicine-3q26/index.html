Huadong Medicine

3Q26 Business Update华东医药

← Research homeBusiness update

Innovative products maintained strong growth, while the aesthetics business remained under pressure.

In 9M26, Huadong Medicine's innovative products and licensing-related revenue reached Rmb2.53bn, up 48.3% YoY, accounting for 21.8% of its pharmaceutical manufacturing revenue. Among key in-licensed products, the PARP inhibitor senaparib (licensed from IMPACT Therapeutics) continued to gain traction following its inclusion in the NRDL, with coverage expanding to nearly 1,200 hospitals and over 450 pharmacies, although product-level sales were not disclosed. Carzey (zevorcabtagene autoleucel), a CAR-T therapy licensed from CARsgen, recorded 169 valid orders in 9M26, including 59 in 3Q26, supported by improving reimbursement accessibility through over 100 supplementary and commercial health insurance plans. Meanwhile, the ustekinumab biosimilar licensed from Qyuns Therapeutics delivered particularly strong growth, with domestic gross sales exceeding Rmb400mn in 9M26, up approximately 100% YoY, and prescription coverage expanding to over 2,000 hospitals. In contrast, the aesthetics business remained weak, with 9M26 revenue declining 42.2% YoY to Rmb906mn, including declines of 54.2% domestically and 31.6% overseas. We calculate that 3Q26 aesthetics revenue fell approximately 44% YoY to Rmb255mn, suggesting limited signs of recovery. Nevertheless, new product launches showed encouraging progress. Rytelo, the company's botulinum toxin product launched in June, has entered over 3,000 aesthetics clinics and generated nearly Rmb100mn in cumulative sales, while MaiLi Precise, a hyaluronic acid filler, received regulatory approval in August. We expect these new products to provide incremental growth opportunities, although their near-term contributions may not be sufficient to fully offset weakness in the existing aesthetics portfolio.
