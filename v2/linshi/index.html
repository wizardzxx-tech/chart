Mindray: Beyond domestic recovery, focus on the duration of global growth and shareholder returns

FY27 fundamental outlook. With channel destocking expected to conclude by end-2026, I expect domestic equipment sales to normalize, supported by the replacement cycle for equipment installed during COVID. However, given Mindray's already high domestic market share in patient monitoring and life support, I see limited scope for the company to meaningfully outgrow the underlying market in these segments. I expect single-digit growth in domestic ultrasound despite potential national VBP, c.10% growth in domestic IVD, c.20% growth in emerging businesses and c.15% international growth. While continued RMB appreciation could weigh on earnings, I think potentially narrower FX losses could allow bottom-line growth to outpace top-line growth in 2027.

Investment positioning and valuation. Given that domestic recovery and double-digit international growth in 2027 are already broadly anticipated by investors, I would position Mindray less as a near-term recovery trade and more as a long-term global market share gain story with increasingly attractive shareholder returns. My reverse DCF calculation suggests that the current valuation implies c.9% FCF CAGR over the next decade, assuming our existing 9% WACC and a 2% terminal growth rate. I think the key debate is therefore not how fast Mindray can grow in 2027, but how long it can sustain close to or above double-digit growth, given its still relatively low global market share and rising international revenue contribution. Meanwhile, assuming a sustained dividend payout ratio of c.65%, a potential FY27 dividend yield of c.3% could make Mindray increasingly attractive from a total shareholder return perspective, even without meaningful multiple expansion.

Catalysts and near-term positioning. One challenge I see for 2027 is the relative lack of major identifiable catalysts, given Mindray's scale and the limited ability of individual product launches to materially change its overall growth trajectory. The potential H-share listing and a possible pre-IPO special dividend could drive near-term investor interest, with Rmb200 widely discussed among investors as a potential share price threshold for the listing to proceed. There is also some investor expectation that management could provide more optimistic FY27 guidance to facilitate the IPO. However, my impression from recent management interactions is that the company does not appear particularly eager to accelerate the listing or raise near-term earnings expectations for this purpose. I would therefore treat the IPO and special dividend as potential event-driven opportunities rather than core elements of the investment thesis. Overall, I am more constructive on Mindray's medium- to long-term compounding potential than on the prospect of a substantial near-term re-rating, which I think will require consistent earnings delivery and further evidence of successful overseas execution.

United Imaging: Strong structural growth, with earnings conversion as the next leg of the investment story

FY27 fundamental outlook. Compared with Mindray, I see greater structural growth potential for United Imaging in both domestic and international markets. Domestically, VBP for Class B large medical equipment appears to be supporting demand for PET/CT and LINAC, while the company has significant room for market share gains in DSA and ultrasound. Its DSA products, particularly their low-radiation capabilities, have gained market recognition and supported rapid market share gains. Increasing acceptance of its ultra-high-end CT and MRI products among leading hospitals should also support growth in its core product lines. Internationally, given its competitive product portfolio and still-low market share, I think 40%+ revenue growth remains achievable from a relatively low base. Overall, I am comfortable with the 20% revenue growth target implied by its equity incentive plan and see potential upside to market expectations.

Earnings conversion. While investors are generally confident in United Imaging's revenue growth, I think the bigger debate is whether such growth can translate into consistent bottom-line delivery, given historical earnings volatility driven by high R&D intensity and FX losses. I see several potential drivers of margin expansion going forward. First, its expanding installed base should support a rising contribution from recurring, higher-margin service revenue, reducing its dependence on new equipment sales. Second, management indicated in our previous discussions that absolute R&D spending would continue to increase but at a slower pace than revenue, allowing for operating leverage. Third, the company's FX hedging initiatives could help mitigate earnings volatility, although the actual financial impact remains uncertain. Taken together, I see scope for earnings growth to increasingly outpace revenue growth.

Investment positioning and valuation. I would position United Imaging as a structural growth story with emerging operating leverage. While its relatively high PE remains a key investor concern, I think its valuation should be assessed against its medium-term earnings growth potential rather than near-term PE alone. On a PEG basis, I see potential for a more attractive valuation relative to Mindray and global imaging peers such as GE HealthCare, Siemens Healthineers and Philips, subject to further analysis. In my view, the key catalyst for 2027 is not necessarily further acceleration in revenue growth, but more consistent earnings delivery and evidence of sustainable margin expansion. I am relatively constructive on the stock given the potential combination of sustained structural revenue growth and improving earnings conversion.

SNIBE: A pure-play beneficiary of IVD normalization and structural market share gains

FY27 fundamental outlook. I expect SNIBE's domestic and international growth trajectories to be broadly similar to those of Mindray's IVD business. However, as a pure-play IVD company without the drag from slower-growing equipment businesses, SNIBE could deliver stronger overall revenue and earnings growth than Mindray. Domestically, the recently announced medical testing fee adjustments in several provinces have been more moderate than feared, while management expects c.20% testing volume growth in 2027, supported by continued market share gains. Internationally, I think c.15% growth remains achievable despite geopolitical and FX headwinds. More importantly, management has indicated that overseas reagent revenue per installed instrument has been steadily increasing. Together with continued new instrument placements, this should support sustainable recurring revenue growth, while a rising reagent revenue contribution could drive further improvement in overseas gross margins, which have already been trending upward.

Key investor debates. Compared with Mindray, I think SNIBE's investors are more directly focused on the evolution of the domestic IVD industry. I see three key areas of debate for 2027: (1) whether further pricing or testing demand pressure could emerge from DRG 3.0, testing fee adjustments in additional provinces and VBP renewals; (2) whether continued domestic market share gains can offset pricing pressure and support sustained revenue growth; and (3) whether international growth can be sustained at c.15%, supported by both installed base expansion and higher reagent consumption per instrument, despite geopolitical and FX headwinds. While the company continues to expand its product portfolio, I do not see any individual product launch materially changing its overall growth trajectory in the near term.

Investment positioning and valuation. I would position SNIBE as a direct beneficiary of IVD normalization and structural market share gains, with an increasingly attractive recurring revenue profile overseas. With some of the pricing concerns already easing, I think the investment focus should gradually shift from policy-related downside risks to earnings delivery. My preliminary reverse DCF calculation suggests that the current valuation implies c.9-10% FCF CAGR over the next decade, assuming a 9% WACC and a 2% terminal growth rate. I see potential upside to this implied growth trajectory, supported by continued domestic market share gains, an expanding overseas installed base, rising reagent revenue per instrument and improving overseas profitability. In the absence of major product catalysts, I expect stock performance in 2027 to be driven primarily by consistent earnings delivery, further policy clarity and evidence of sustainable and increasingly profitable international growth.

ICLs (Kingmed and Dian): Margin recovery without a convincing top-line inflection

FY27 fundamental outlook. Unlike IVD manufacturers, I think the better-than-feared medical testing fee cuts announced in several provinces may be less constructive for ICLs than initially expected. While more moderate price cuts could alleviate direct pricing pressure and allow ICLs to retain more favorable pricing terms with hospitals, they may also weaken hospitals' economic incentives to outsource testing. I see four key revenue drivers for the ICL industry: ASP, underlying testing demand, outsourcing penetration and the adoption of new testing items by hospitals. With ASP likely to remain under single-digit annual pressure, limited visibility on outsourcing penetration and slower adoption of new testing items amid the medical anti-corruption campaign, I remain cautious on the pace of top-line recovery in 2027. On the positive side, the prolonged industry downturn and delayed collection of COVID-related receivables have led to the exit of smaller players, supporting industry consolidation and gross margin recovery at both Kingmed and Dian.

Growth visibility and alternative growth drivers. I see increasing challenges to the revenue targets under Kingmed's equity incentive plan, which imply annual growth of 6%/8%/10% in 2026-28. Adicon's five-year strategic plan, targeting c.11% organic revenue CAGR and over Rmb6bn in total revenue by 2031, also provides a useful industry reference. However, I remain uncertain whether growth in esoteric testing and other emerging businesses will be sufficient to offset continued pressure on routine testing. The termination of the Crown Bioscience acquisition also makes Adicon's overall revenue target more challenging. Both Kingmed and Dian have been actively adopting AI to improve operating efficiency and diagnostic service quality, including AI-assisted pathology and proprietary diagnostic models. While I see potential for further cost savings, I have yet to see a clear path toward meaningful commercial monetization of their accumulated data assets.

Investment positioning and rating implications. Our original investment thesis for Kingmed was largely based on an anticipated industry inflection driven by both rising market concentration and higher outsourcing penetration. While the former appears to be materializing, I am now less confident in the latter, particularly following the more moderate-than-expected testing fee adjustments. I would therefore position ICLs as margin recovery stories with limited top-line growth visibility, rather than structural growth recovery plays at this stage. My impression is that investor interest remains relatively muted, partly because the initial bottoming-out thesis has already been explored and the next leg of growth is less visible. Given the weakening of a key assumption behind our original investment thesis and the lack of identifiable near-term catalysts, I think it is worth revisiting our ratings for both Kingmed and Dian, including potential downgrades, pending a reassessment of our earnings forecasts and valuations.
