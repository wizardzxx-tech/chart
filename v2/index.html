China IVD & ICL

Industry Update

DomesticInternational

Domestic market: The IVD and ICL industries are showing signs of a gradual recovery, with easing pricing pressure, although the outlook for ICL outsourcing penetration remains uncertain.

Based on our recent discussions with management teams from Mindray, Snibe, AmoyDx and KingMed, the negative impact of regulatory measures, including IVD VBP, DRG payment reform, unbundling of diagnostic testing packages and anti-corruption initiatives, appears to be gradually fading. The recently announced adjustments to medical laboratory testing fees across several provinces have been more moderate than previously expected, with prices for some tests remaining unchanged or even increasing. Companies generally expect pricing pressure in 2027 to ease significantly compared with the past two years. Specifically, Snibe expects its overall reagent ASP decline to remain within 5% in 2027, Mindray estimates the impact on its ex-factory prices to be less than 10%, while KingMed estimates the static revenue impact from the latest pricing reform to be within 5%. In terms of testing volumes, the industry remains in a bottoming-out phase. Snibe expects industry testing volumes per instrument to decline 4-5% in 2026 before stabilizing in 2027, while Mindray believes that adjustments under DRG 3.0 could help alleviate previous pressure on testing demand. Despite a potentially modest recovery in overall industry demand, leading IVD manufacturers may continue to outperform through domestic substitution and market share gains. Mindray reported over 20% growth in domestic IVD testing volumes in 2026, while Snibe targets approximately 15% growth in its own testing volumes in 2027. For the ICL sector, KingMed believes that the negative impact of regulatory policies has largely played out and testing demand should gradually recover. However, we believe the more moderate-than-expected reduction in testing fees, while alleviating direct pricing pressure on ICL providers, could also weaken hospitals' economic incentives to outsource testing services. As such, whether the previously anticipated acceleration in outsourcing penetration will materialize remains uncertain. Outsourcing should remain economically attractive for hospitals with insufficient testing volumes or limited economies of scale, but broader penetration gains may take longer to materialize. Looking ahead, ICL growth may increasingly depend on a recovery in underlying testing demand, expansion into county-level integrated healthcare networks, and incremental demand generated by more affordable diagnostic testing offerings.

International market: Overseas IVD markets continue to offer meaningful growth opportunities, particularly in Europe and Latin America, while an increasing reagent revenue contribution could support long-term profitability.

Leading Chinese IVD manufacturers remain optimistic about the long-term growth potential of international markets, which continue to represent an important growth driver. Mindray indicated that its European IVD business has maintained strong growth since 2024, supported by increasing penetration into high-end hospitals and laboratories, with further market share gains expected. Meanwhile, reagents currently account for approximately 50% of Mindray's overseas IVD revenue, with management seeing potential for this proportion to reach 80-90% over the longer term. As the installed instrument base generates recurring reagent sales, the overseas IVD business could benefit from an improving revenue mix and profitability. Snibe reported 12% YoY overseas revenue growth in 3Q26, with Europe, Latin America and Africa growing 29%, 20% and 18%, respectively. However, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East disrupted shipments, distributor expansion and hospital procurement, resulting in slower overall growth than initially targeted. Management nevertheless indicated that most major international markets outside the Middle East continued to deliver healthy growth. AmoyDx, following adjustments to its overseas team and distribution channels, expects its international business to return to approximately 20% annual growth over the coming years, primarily driven by PCR- and NGS-based companion diagnostic products. In Japan, an established reimbursement framework supports testing demand, with volumes continuing to grow despite reported revenue pressure from yen depreciation. In Europe, AmoyDx sees opportunities to expand its presence in major hospitals and central laboratories through registered products and local distributors, while Southeast Asia remains at an early stage of molecular cancer diagnostics adoption, offering substantial long-term potential. Overall, we believe overseas IVD markets continue to offer meaningful opportunities for Chinese manufacturers through market share gains and increasing diagnostic testing penetration, although near-term performance may vary across regions due to differences in healthcare reimbursement systems, regulatory approval timelines, FX movements and geopolitical developments.
