03 · THE UNIT-ECONOMICS CHECK

Ten times the tests.
Half the reagent?

Automation changes the denominator. Smaller volumes and denser plates can reduce materials per test, even when experimental activity expands. Move quantities and prices separately to see the effect on purchasing.

Price, overhead & usable-well assumptions

Ratios are scenario inputs, not vendor price quotes. Compare equivalent product specifications and usable volumes.

Extra reagent is modeled relative to usable assay volume in each case. Actual dead volume depends on reservoirs, dispensing and sample-handling workflows. Baseline plates assume 100% usable wells.

Baseline: 100k tests · 100 µL · 96 wells · 1 tip/test.
Default: 100% usable wells, no extra reagent, 1× prices. Controls, retests, source plates, services and instrument CAPEX require separate inputs.

QUANTITIES & SPEND · BASELINE = 1×

10× more experiments, but only 0.5× the reagent volume.

Net purchasing∝Experimental activity×Materials per test×Net price & mix

A browser-local illustrative calculation, not an AI adoption forecast or a revenue model. Plate spending exceeds the original example’s baseline only if comparable plate ASP is above 1.60×. More expensive high-density plates are a possibility, not an established price assumption. Average tips/test models a workflow and does not recommend reusing biological-sample tips.

FIGURE 13 · LABEL GUIDE

Nine labels.
Nine different questions.

What the antibody matrix measures, in plain English. Real measurements use different units and assay conditions; they are not nine interchangeable scores or necessarily nine separate experiments.

KD

Equilibrium dissociation constant

How tightly does the antibody bind its target?

M, nM or pM

Lower KD means tighter binding under matched conditions. In a simple 1:1 model, KD = koff / kon.

SPR / BLI; model, valency and temperature matter.

kon

Association rate constant

How quickly does a binding complex form?

M⁻¹ s⁻¹

A larger kon means faster association at the same concentration. It is not a direct measure of clinical onset.

SPR / BLI association curves.

koff

Dissociation rate constant

How quickly does the antibody let go?

s⁻¹

A smaller koff means slower dissociation; ideal 1:1 residence time is 1 / koff. This is not serum half-life or a universal efficacy predictor.

SPR / BLI dissociation curves.

expr

Expression yield

Can the cell system make the antibody, and how much?

mg/L or µg/mL

Yield affects sample supply and production feasibility. Host, format, culture conditions and recovery must be recorded.

Expression cultures, titer assays and purification.

Tm

Thermal unfolding transition

How resistant is the protein to unfolding when heated?

°C

A higher transition temperature suggests greater thermal stability in the tested conditions. It does not directly establish shelf life or in-vivo half-life.

nanoDSF / DSF; IgGs can show multiple transitions.

agg

Aggregation

Do molecules form larger clusters or particles?

SEC % monomer / % HMW; method-specific readouts

Aggregation can affect formulation and immunogenicity risk. The result depends on concentration, stress and the measurement method.

SEC-HPLC, light scattering and particle analysis.

poly

Polyreactivity

Does the antibody bind several unrelated non-target materials?

Assay signal, ratio or score

Flags non-specific binding and developability risk. There is no universal score that guarantees clinical failure.

Defined non-target binding panels and controls.

epi

Epitope

Where on the target does the antibody bind?

Competition bin, residues or structural site

Binding location helps interpret mechanism. A competition bin is not a precise structural epitope or proof of functional effect.

SPR / BLI binning, mutagenesis or structural mapping.

cell

Cell potency

Does the antibody produce the intended effect in living cells?

EC50 / IC50 plus Emax and assay context

Measures functional activity in the chosen cell model. Target expression, timing and maximum response matter; it does not replace in-vivo or clinical evidence.

Reporter assays, flow cytometry and functional cell readouts.

How to read the heatmap

Figure 13 uses invented integers from 1 to 10,000 on a common linear color scale. Darker blue means a larger displayed number. The values have no biological units and are not real measurements, assay coverage or a drug-quality ranking. In real records, epitope is usually a site, category or competition bin.

REVIEW UPDATE · 13 OCT 2026

What the evidence
supports.

The review strengthens the mechanism while reducing reliance on universal molecule counts, invented customer shares and assumed capital capture.

Selection can reduce synthesis

Classical docking screened 170 million compounds in 2019. DSP-1181 and REC-617 illustrate smaller, selected synthesis programs. These are study/program examples, not matched per-drug cohorts.

Lyu et al. 2019 ↗ · Sumitomo technical report ↗ · Recursion annual report ↗

Protein screens have different units

Pooled library screens, expressed clones, purified proteins and full functional profiles are distinct workloads. Shared assays can generate multiple labels. The demand variable is useful measurement depth and net inputs per funded workflow.

Bennett et al. 2023 ↗

Funding is not vendor revenue

Personnel, trials, service labor, data/compute, facilities and cash not spent on tools absorb funding. Figure 07 now uses 5% / 10% / 20% purchase-capture sensitivities, with no claimed empirical allocation.

Recursion 2025 10-K ↗

Clinical gains still need proof

7.9% and 13.8% are historical Phase I-to-approval estimates from different samples and methods. They are neither a confidence interval nor an AI comparison. Early-phase success does not establish eventual approval gains.

BIO report ↗ · Wong et al. ↗
